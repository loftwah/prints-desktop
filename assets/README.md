## Image direction

The Last Horizon depicts an ivory concrete ring and vermilion sun above a
volcanic coast, with a still-water reflection, ink-plum shadows and 35mm grain.
The contact sheet extends the same visual approach across copper dunes, pink
salt flats, a cobalt mountain pass, a moss-covered airlock and flooded concrete
slabs. Five of its six images were cropped into individual square prints.

## Generation prompts

**The Last Horizon.** Create one full-bleed landscape photograph. A research
station on a dark slate volcanic island, a huge pale ivory concrete ring
turned on edge frames a brilliant vermilion sun just above the horizon, long
exposure still water below reflecting a thin burnt-orange line, remote rock
foreground right, layered rising mist, deeply atmospheric ink-plum shadows.
Give it the feel of an analogue large-format photograph of an impossible
sculpture, with fine grain, luminous cold lilac sky and dark water below.
Place the circle slightly left of centre. Use a landscape composition
without lettering, people, logos or borders.

**Afterlight contact sheet.** Create a seamless three-column by two-row grid
of six independent analogue speculative landscape photographs with no gaps or
frames. Top row shows a red antenna tower in copper dunes, a fractured
salmon-pink salt pan with a single black rock, and a cobalt mountain pass with
a distant lavender beacon. Bottom row shows an overgrown concrete airlock, a
flooded purple plain with concrete echo chambers, and a volcanic coast with a
concrete ring centred around an orange sun. Keep the subjects central in
their cells, with photographic light, fine grain and no lettering or people.
